Overview: this is Stage 1 of 2

This scanner's whole job is to shrink a universe of hundreds of tickers down to a short list worth actually looking at. It is not a buy signal by itself.

Stage 1 (this scanner): scores the whole market nightly on the numeric shape of a setup — momentum, tightness, volume, extension. Stage 2 (TradingView + the companion Pine indicator): you look at the actual chart for each candidate and judge the things a number can't capture — is the consolidation genuinely orderly, is this a young trend or an aging one, does price action look coiled or sloppy.

Stage 2's indicator now shows these same 0-100 scores directly on the chart (Breakout Score, EP Score, P-Short Score rows in its status table), computed with the same tier thresholds as this scanner — so a ticker's score here should match what you see on the chart for the same bar. The boolean SIGNAL markers (BO/EP/P-SHORT/P-LONG) are a separate, stricter all-or-nothing gate on top of the score, not a replacement for it — a ticker can score well without the marker firing.

Every trader who has built something like this — from Stockbee's Pradeep Bonde (whose Momentum Burst / Episodic Pivot ideas this setup borrows from) to the various TradingView/ChartMill implementations — says the same thing: a numeric scan finds candidates shaped like the setup, it doesn't confirm the setup. Chart confirmation in Stage 2 is not optional.

How to use this, end to end

The scanner and the indicator are two steps of one habit, not two separate tools. A typical session looks like this:

  1. Run the scanner after the close. It scores the whole liquidity-filtered universe and writes out the CSV + HTML dashboard, sorted by score within each setup.
  2. Scan the dashboard top-down, one setup at a time. Prioritize SETUP! and WATCH names first — Monitor-tier names are worth a glance but rarely worth acting on same-day. Also check the "Recently Triggered" table for anything that fired in the last few days and might still be actionable, even if it's no longer scoring high today.
  3. Open each candidate on TradingView with the Pine indicator loaded. Confirm the score in the status table roughly matches what you saw in the dashboard for that ticker.
  4. Walk the chart checklist: badge/score, 2LYNCH count if it's a Breakout candidate, the shape of the Consolidation/Linearity/Parabolic boxes (see the sections below for how to read each), and the market regime badge.
  5. If it holds up, plan the trade before anything else — entry trigger, stop level, and size (via the Position Sizing Calculator on the dashboard). See "Entries, stops & exits" below.
  6. If it's not quite there, set an alert instead of forcing it. WATCH/Monitor names are meant to be revisited as they develop, not chased into a lower-quality entry.
  7. Log what you actually did — entry, stop, size, setup type — somewhere outside the tool. The scanner has no memory of which signals you acted on or how they played out.

This loop is meant to run nightly for the scan, and live/intraday only for confirming an already-flagged candidate — not for discovering new ideas by staring at random charts during the day.

Badge tiers

Every setup gets a 0-100 score instead of a plain pass/fail, so you can see "close but not quite" candidates, not just fully-confirmed ones.

A high score means the numbers line up. It doesn't mean the chart looks clean — always look before you act, especially on WATCH/Monitor names where something is still missing by definition.

Recently Triggered

Sits right below the legend on the dashboard, above the four setup tables. It answers a specific gap: a SETUP!-caliber hit can genuinely disappear from tomorrow's scan even though nothing went wrong — most commonly an Episodic Pivot, whose score is built almost entirely from today's gap and volume (see the Episodic Pivot section below). The day after the gap, there's no new gap and no repeat volume surge, so the score naturally collapses even though the stock may still be perfectly tradeable a day or two later.

This table keeps any ticker that scored 80 or higher on any of the three scored setups — Breakout, Episodic Pivot, or Parabolic Short — visible for a few extra days after the day it actually fired, even once today's live score has dropped back down. It's deliberately limited to SETUP!-caliber hits; WATCH/Monitor-tier scores are never tracked here, even briefly. It does not affect scoring in any way; it's a memory layer sitting on top of scores that are already computed elsewhere.

Market regime badge

SPY's 10-day SMA vs 20-day SMA, shown at the top of the dashboard. Bullish = 10-day above 20-day. Bearish/caution = 10-day below 20-day.

This isn't a hard filter on the scan results — candidates still show up either way — but it's context you should weigh heavily. Breakouts and EPs statistically work much better when the broad market is healthy; several drawdowns in trend-following momentum strategies (including ones Kris himself has talked about) trace back to ignoring a deteriorating market regime and continuing to trade setups at full size anyway. Treat a bearish badge as a signal to trade smaller, be pickier, and expect a higher failure rate.

Liquidity filters (apply to every setup)

Before any setup is even scored, a ticker has to clear basic tradeability bars:

  • ADR% ≥ 3% — average daily range as a percent of price. Low-ADR stocks don't move enough intraday to be worth the setup's risk/reward; this is the single most Qullamaggie-specific filter here.
  • Avg dollar volume ≥ $5M — filters out stocks too thin to actually get in and out of at size.
  • Price ≥ $2 — very low-priced stocks behave differently (wider relative spreads, more manipulation-prone) and usually aren't the kind of name this setup family targets.

These thresholds are conservative defaults, not gospel — they live in the DEFAULTS dict near the top of the script if you want to loosen or tighten them.

Breakout score (0-100)

Rewards a stock that already made a real move, pulled back into an orderly base hugging its moving averages, and is now pushing higher again on volume.

BucketMax ptsWhat it's checkingWhat to look out for
Momentum35 1-month and 3-month price return A huge score here with a flat/messy chart usually means the "move" was one or two gap days, not a real trend — open the chart before trusting this alone.
Trend Position20 EMA10 is rising (not just "above") AND full bullish MA stack (EMA10 > EMA20 > SMA50) Deliberately stricter than a flat "close above the MA" check — a stock barely poking above a flat EMA10 gets zero credit here now, only genuine slope + alignment counts. (Mirrors the same slope/stack logic Minervini's Trend Template uses, which Kris's own approach draws on.)
MA Surfing25 How tightly price is hugging the 10/20-day EMA This is the "orderly consolidation" proxy. High points here + a chart that's actually choppy (not genuinely tight) is exactly what the 2LYNCH checks below exist to catch.
Volume & Extension20 Volume multiple vs average, % of 52-week high A stock already at 90%+ of its 52-week high scores well here — but check it's not already extended 5+ days into the move with no pause left.
The Breakout score alone can be fooled by a stock that's just been trending smoothly upward for months with no real base — it'll score high on Momentum/Trend/Surfing without ever having a genuine "breakout" moment. This is exactly what the 2LYNCH checklist below is for.

2LYNCH consolidation-quality checklist

Borrowed from Pradeep Bonde ("Stockbee") — the trader whose Momentum Burst and Episodic Pivot ideas Qullamaggie has credited as the origin of his own EP setup. Where the 0-100 score above measures momentum and position, 2LYNCH specifically judges consolidation quality — the difference between a clean, low-risk base and a sloppy one that just happens to score well numerically.

Only shown on the Breakout table. Each ticker gets a count out of 6 (lynch_count) and a string showing exactly which letters passed (lynch_flags, e.g. "2LNH" means it passed 2/L/N/H but failed Y/C).

Short answer: yes, more is better. All 6 passing is the cleanest possible consolidation by this checklist's definition — you're not trying to hit some specific subset, you want as many letters lit up as possible. See the "How to use lynch_count in practice" tiers below for rough guidance on what different counts mean in terms of chart quality.
2 Not already up two days in a row going into today

Avoids chasing a move that's already run two straight green days — entries right after a 2-day thrust have worse follow-through than entries after a pause.

Fail looks like: two consecutive green candles immediately before today's bar, with today making it a third.

Pass looks like: today follows a flat/red day, or is itself only the first up-day after the base.

L Linearity of the prior move (efficiency ratio ≥ 0.35)

A move that went mostly in one direction with few big reversals is a "clean trend" — easier to judge, easier to hold. A jagged, back-and-forth move with the same net gain is much lower quality even though the raw % return looks identical.

Fail looks like: a saw-tooth chart, big up days wiped out by big down days, net progress only from a couple of outlier candles.

Pass looks like: a staircase — mostly steady higher lows and higher highs with shallow pullbacks.

Y Young trend (hasn't been above its 50-day SMA for more than ~90 bars)

The 1st or 2nd breakout out of a base is statistically a better risk/reward than the 4th or 5th — each subsequent breakout attempt out of an aging trend has more overhead supply and more exhausted buyers above it.

Fail looks like: the stock has been trending for the better part of a year already and this is yet another breakout attempt deep into an extended run.

Pass looks like: a relatively fresh trend, ideally still within its first few months off the lows.

N Narrow-range or down day immediately before the trigger

Wants to see the stock coiling — volatility contracting — right before it breaks out, not already thrusting hard the day before (which suggests the move already started without you).

Fail looks like: yesterday was already a big, wide-range up day — today's "breakout" is really day two of a move already underway.

Pass looks like: yesterday was quiet, narrow, or slightly red — a coiled spring, not an already-released one.

C Consolidation quality (≤1 sharp ≥4% down day inside the base, plus volume dried up vs. the prior move)

This is the core "orderly base" check. A base riddled with sharp down days is one where weak hands are still getting shaken out violently — not the calm, low-volume drift that precedes the best breakouts.

Fail looks like: multiple red days of 4%+ inside the base, and volume during the base is still as heavy as during the original move (no drying up).

Pass looks like: a quiet, boring, low-volume sideways drift with at most one rough day.

H Closes in the top 30% of the trigger bar's range

A breakout that closes near its high shows buyers were in control into the close. A breakout that closes in the lower half of its range — even if it technically broke out intraday — is a much weaker signal and more likely to fail the next day.

Fail looks like: a long upper wick, price pushed higher intraday then faded hard into the close.

Pass looks like: a strong close at or near the day's high.

How to use lynch_count in practice

  • 5-6/6 — high-quality, textbook-shaped setup. Worth prioritizing over a same-scored candidate with a lower count.
  • 3-4/6 — mixed. Open the chart and see which specific letters failed and whether that matters for your read of the setup.
  • 0-2/6 — the shape is probably wrong even if the 0-100 score looks decent. This is the classic "numbers say breakout, chart says something else" case.

Run the scanner with --strict to automatically require lynch_count ≥ 4 before a Breakout signal counts as confirmed — a meaningfully more aggressive filter than the raw score alone.

2LYNCH is a numeric approximation of a chart-judgment checklist, same as everything else here. A stock can pass 6/6 and still have a bad chart for reasons these specific checks don't cover (e.g. a huge round-number resistance level right above, or an earnings date landing inside your hold period). It narrows the pile, it doesn't replace looking.

Episodic Pivot score (0-100)

Rewards a large, catalyst-driven gap on heavy volume, especially when the stock wasn't already extended going in.

BucketMax ptsWhat it's checkingWhat to look out for
Gap/Move40Size of today's gap Bigger isn't always better — a 30%+ gap on a small/illiquid name can be as much a trap as an opportunity. Check what actually caused it (earnings, guidance, FDA news, etc.) before assuming it's tradeable.
Volume Surge25Volume vs. average Confirms institutional participation, not just retail chasing a headline.
Prior Base20How flat the prior 3 months were (magnitude of move, either direction) A quiet base before the gap means this is a genuine surprise, not a continuation of an already-known story. Breakout15Is price now above its 20-day high Confirms the gap actually cleared recent resistance, not just a big move still trapped in a range.
EPs are event-driven — the catalyst matters as much as the numbers. Always check why the gap happened before trusting the score. A gap on genuine positive news behaves very differently from a gap on a rumor or a small-float short squeeze.

Parabolic Short score (0-100)

Rewards an extreme, fast, overextended move — the kind that eventually mean-reverts hard.

BucketMax ptsWhat it's checkingWhat to look out for
Massive Gain351-month return The higher this scores, the more "unsustainable" the move looks numerically — but unsustainable moves can keep going for a while before they crack. This is not a timing signal by itself.
Consecutive Up Days25Up-day streak length Long streaks (7+) are rare and historically associated with exhaustion, but shorting into strength purely on streak length is one of the highest-risk trades in this entire scanner.
Extension from MA25% above the 10-day SMA Measures how far price has stretched from its own short-term trend — the further, the more likely (not certain) a snapback.
Volatility15ADR% High-ADR names swing hard both ways — this setup needs that volatility to work, but it also means outsized risk if you're on the wrong side of a continuation.
Shorting parabolic moves is widely considered the highest-risk setup in this entire family, Qullamaggie included — squeezes can extend far past what looks "rational," and a high score here is really flagging "extreme," not "about to reverse." Size accordingly, and treat this table as the last one to trade off of, not the first.

Parabolic Long (post-crash reversal)

A boolean signal only — no 0-100 score defined for this one. Fires when a stock had a genuine "crash" (steep drop, consecutive down days, stretched far below its MA) within the last few bars, and today shows a green reversal candle.

This is the least well-defined setup in the scanner — the source material describes it qualitatively rather than with hard numeric rules, so this is a heuristic approximation more than the other three. Treat any hit here as a starting point for chart review, not a confirmed candidate.

Entries, stops & exits

The scanner and indicator find candidates — everything below is what to actually do once one clears. This is Kris's own stated trade-management approach, condensed and mapped onto this tool's setups. None of it is enforced in code; it's judgment you apply on top of a badge.

The rule underneath all of this

Cut losers fast, let winners run. Every entry, stop, and exit rule below exists to enforce that one asymmetry — a small, fast, well-defined loss on the trades that don't work, against an open-ended trail on the ones that do.

Entry timing, by setup

SetupTypical triggerNotes
Breakout The breakout bar itself, as price clears the consolidation high on above-average volume — or the next bar if you missed the exact break Don't chase a breakout that's already extended several days past the actual break. That's the 2LYNCH "Y: young trend" check failing for a reason.
Episodic Pivot The gap day itself, or the first tight 1-3 day pause immediately after the gap Entering into the gap directly is higher risk/reward than waiting for the pause afterward; waiting risks missing the move if price never comes back down to a buyable level.
Parabolic Short Not the high score by itself — wait for the first real break of trend (a close below a short EMA, or the first sharp red day after extreme extension) Shorting strength directly, on score alone, is how this setup blows accounts up. The score flags a candidate; it is not an entry signal.
Parabolic Long The reversal candle itself (green close after the crash + stretch conditions), or next-day confirmation The least-defined setup in the tool (see the Parabolic Long section above) — treat entries here as higher-uncertainty than the other three.

Stops

Kris's stated stop rule is a specific price level, not a fixed percentage or multiple: the low of the breakout/entry day, or just below the low of the consolidation/base that preceded it — whichever gives the tighter, more defensible risk. If that stop implies more risk than your sizing rules allow, the answer is to size down or skip the trade, not to move the stop further away.

The dashboard's Position Sizing Calculator currently uses an ADR%-multiple as a proxy for this stop distance, since that's the only figure already computed for every row. It is not the literal rule above — it's a convenient stand-in. Treat the calculator's Stop column as a rough sizing estimate, and set your actual stop from the chart (low of day / low of base) before placing the trade.

Sizing

Covered in more detail on the dashboard itself, but as a reminder: risk 0.25-1% of account per trade (up to roughly 1.5% on smaller accounts), and never let a single position's value exceed about 30% of the account overnight. The Position Sizing Calculator does this math live once you know your real stop distance.

Taking profit & trailing

Once a trade is working, the general approach is to bank part of the gain into the first big vertical move — locking in some strength rather than waiting for a pullback that might not come — then trail the remainder using a moving-average break as the exit signal. The 10-day EMA gives the fastest, tightest trail; the 20-day EMA gives a looser one that rides out more noise. Either way, exit the remaining position when price closes decisively below whichever MA you're using to trail. There's no fixed profit target — the trail is what lets a genuinely strong move keep running instead of capping it artificially.

Invalidation — when to just get out

  • Breakout / EP: a close back below the breakout level or the low of the base means the setup failed. Exit — don't average down or "give it room."
  • Parabolic Short: a new high after you're in means the squeeze isn't done. Cover, don't hold through it hoping for a top.
  • Parabolic Long: a failure to hold the reversal (a new low below the reversal-day low) means the crash wasn't actually done bottoming.

Common pitfalls

  • High score ≠ confirmed setup. The 0-100 scores measure momentum/position/volume shape. They don't (fully) measure chart quality — that's what 2LYNCH is for on Breakout, and what your own eyes are for everywhere else.
  • SEC-sourced universe is noisier than Nasdaq Trader's. If your scan used the SEC fallback source, a handful of OTC/foreign-listed names can slip through even after cleanup — the liquidity filters catch most of them, but double-check anything unfamiliar.
  • Delayed/EOD data. This is not a real-time feed — treat every candidate as "worth checking," not "actionable right now," especially intraday.
  • Market regime matters more than any single score. The same score means something different in a bullish vs. bearish tape — see the Market Regime section above.
  • This is Stage 1 only. Always confirm on the actual chart (Stage 2) before acting — see the Overview section above.
